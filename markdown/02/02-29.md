## Lives of the Saints

### 1. VENERABLE JOHN CASSIAN

This great spiritual father was born in Rome to distinguished parents. In his youth he mastered all the worldly sciences, especially philosophy and astronomy. He then gave himself entirely to the study of Holy Scripture. Pressing onward from good to better and desiring ever higher degrees of perfection, Cassian departed from Rome to Constantinople in order to hear and see Saint John Chrysostom in person. Chrysostom instructed him and ordained him deacon. Having benefited greatly from the wise Chrysostom, Cassian set out further to the East, to learn and perfect himself still more. He lingered longest in Egypt, in Nitria, among the renowned spiritual athletes, from whom he learned the practice of every virtue. At last he returned again to the West and settled in the city of Marseille. There he founded two monasteries, one for men and one for women. At the request of the monks, Cassian wrote several works, of which especially useful for lovers of the spiritual life is: "Eight Books on the Struggle Against the Eight Principal Passions." Highly important is also his work against the heretic Nestorius, written at the request of the Archdeacon Leo. He served the Lord faithfully and enriched many with wisdom, then departed to eternal life in the year 435. The relics of Saint Cassian rest to this day in Marseille.

### 2. VENERABLE BARSANUPHIUS

Barsanuphius was a native of Palestine. Born a pagan, he was baptized at the age of eighteen and was immediately tonsured as a monk, receiving the name John. When he became renowned for his virtuous life, he was elected archbishop of Damascus. But he did not remain long in that position. Longing for solitary spiritual struggle, he secretly left Damascus and departed to the Nitrian desert. There he presented himself as the monk Barsanuphius and was immediately given the obedience of serving as the monastery's water-carrier. The former archbishop received that obedience with joy. By his wise discernment, meekness, and diligence, Barsanuphius soon became an exemplary model for all the monks. Only before his death was it revealed to the monks who Barsanuphius had been. And so this saint served by his example as a rebuke to the proud and power-loving, and as a consolation to the humble and meek. He reposed peacefully and departed to the Lord in the year 457.

## Hymn of Praise

### VENERABLE JOHN CASSIAN

Besides the dreadful passions, Cassian also enumerated  
A tenth — impure thoughts.  
Gluttony first, in drink and in food,  
Then fornication — the second poison to both spirit and body,

Avarice — the shackle that binds a man to metal,  
Anger — the frost of the human heart that freezes and constricts.  
Despondency — the insatiable worm that gnaws the soul,  
Sloth — the drowsiness that spins death for the soul,

Vainglory — a serpent, a many-headed serpent,  
Everywhere and nowhere — hidden by the grass,  
Pride — a two-edged sword that cuts and hews,  
And mercilessly destroys both young and old,

Young men vigorous, arrogant in their strength,  
And spiritual elders, precious to themselves.  
Against all these passions God is our defense  
Through the holy prayers of Saint Cassian.

## Reflection

Saint John Cassian writes of the struggle against the spirit of fornication as follows: "The struggle with the spirit of fornication is a fierce struggle, longer than the others, constant, and brought to complete victory by only a small number of men. This struggle begins from the first age of maturity and does not cease until all the other passions have been conquered. In this struggle a twofold weapon is necessary. For the attainment of perfect and pure continence, bodily fasting alone is not sufficient (though it is above all necessary); along with it is required contrition of spirit and unceasing prayer against this most impure spirit; then constant instruction from Holy Scripture combined with spiritual activity; then bodily labor and handicraft, which keeps the heart from wandering and restores it to itself; and above all, deep and true humility, without which victory can never be gained over any passion whatsoever. Victory over this passion is conditioned upon the perfect cleansing of the heart, from which, by the words of the Lord, the poison of this disease also flows. For out of the heart proceed evil thoughts... adulteries, fornications, etc. (Matt. 15:19)... One must have steadfast humility in the heart and patience, as well as careful self-preservation during the day from anger and the other passions. For to the degree that the fire of anger enters into us, to that degree the heat of lust more easily penetrates into us afterward."

It is noteworthy that many other great spiritual fathers also draw a causal connection between the passion of anger and the passion of lustful desire, from which it follows that the wrathful are the most lustful.

## Contemplation

Contemplate the Lord Jesus as the vigilant watchman over His Church (Matt. 28:20), namely:

1. How He keeps watch over the entire created world, and especially over His Church, purchased with His blood;
2. How He keeps watch over every baptized soul as a gardener over his crop;
3. How He leads His Church through silence and through storm, guiding her to her final victory;
4. How He keeps watch also over my own life, that it may grow and be built up into His eternal Kingdom.

## Homily

### on the living presence of Christ

**Lo, I am with you always, even unto the end of the world** (Matt. 28:20)

Behold the consolation of consolations!

Behold a consolation for those whom the storm batters! Let them only remember: Christ is there beside them, and let them not fear. He is the helmsman.

Behold a consolation for those who grow old! Let them not lose sight of the fact that Christ journeys with them through time all the way into eternity and into eternal youth, and let them enter peacefully.

Behold a consolation for those whom men torment! Let them not think that they are abandoned, for Christ is with them in all their torments, and in the court, and in the prison — and let them rejoice. He is the Judge.

Behold a consolation for those whom evil spirits trouble! Let them understand that Christ, the Conqueror of evil spirits, is on their side, and let them take courage. He is the Victor.

Behold a consolation for all those who seek the light of righteousness and truth! Let them believe that Christ is nearer to their soul than their own eyes, and let them hold fast to His guidance. He is the Light.

O my brethren, truly Christ is unceasingly with us as light is unceasingly with eyes that see. But O our grief — how the eyes of our souls are shut, and the light labors in vain to meet the pupils of our sight! O our sorrow and grief, when we are not with Christ!

He goes out to meet us — do we go out to meet Him? He wills to be with us — do we will to be with Him? If we desire consolation, we must be with Him all the days until the end of our own age.

O Lord, our only consolation, forsake us not! To Thee be glory and praise forever. Amen.
